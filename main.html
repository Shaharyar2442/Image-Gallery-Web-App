About Gothic Architecture

Gothic architecture is a style of architecture that flourished in Europe during the High and Late Middle Ages. It evolved from Romanesque architecture and was succeeded by Renaissance architecture. Originating in 12th-century France, it was widely used for cathedrals and churches until the 16th century.

Its most prominent features include the use of the pointed arch, the ribbed vault, and the flying buttress. These structural engineering achievements allowed for taller, lighter buildings with larger windows, leading to the extensive use of stained glass which filled the interiors with light and color.

This gallery is dedicated to preserving the memory and beauty of these magnificent structures. We believe that Gothic architecture represents a pinnacle of human creativity and devotion.
